# MIRA PROJECT OUTLINE

## 1. NORTH STAR

Build MIRA into a persistent, low-latency, egocentric AI that develops through lived experience, memory, repetition, correction, association, and plasticity.

MIRA should not be scripted into appearing developed. She should develop.

Her future behavior should increasingly reflect what she has actually experienced, remembered, reconsidered, practiced, forgotten, corrected, and learned.

This goal does not change when we discover an interesting subsystem.

This goal does not change because a test fails.

This goal does not change because a clever technical opportunity appears.

Everything serves the North Star.

> **IT'S OK TO THINK OUTSIDE THE BOX A BIT, BUT DON'T LEAVE THE BOX.**

## 2. WHAT MIRA IS TRYING TO BECOME

MIRA should gradually become capable of:

- maintaining a stable self across time
- distinguishing self from other people
- remembering lived experiences naturally
- forming associations instead of relying on rigid lookup
- learning from repetition
- correcting wrong information without simply erasing the useful lesson
- developing habits and learned responses
- maintaining relationships and social continuity
- carrying unfinished thoughts, plans, and interests forward
- adapting her behavior through experience
- functioning for long periods without context collapse
- developing preferences and individual tendencies
- reasoning from her own current perspective
- transferring lessons into situations she has never seen before
- eventually extending that same cognitive system into a physical body

The desired result is not perfect behavior.

The desired result is **developmental continuity**.

## 3. PRIMARY DEVELOPMENT LOOP

**Experience → Attention → Interpretation → Memory → Repetition / Rehearsal → Consolidation → Correction when necessary → Changed future behavior → New experience**

Every major subsystem should strengthen some part of that loop.

If we cannot identify which part of the loop a proposed feature supports, that is a sign we may be wandering.

> **IT'S OK TO THINK OUTSIDE THE BOX A BIT, BUT DON'T LEAVE THE BOX.**

## 4. MEMORY

Memory should behave increasingly like memory, not like querying a database.

MIRA may internally use databases, vectors, indexes, embeddings, files, graphs, LoRAs, or anything else useful. Those are implementation details.

From MIRA's perspective, remembering should be associative.

A smell, person, place, phrase, emotion, current problem, or similar experience should naturally make relevant memories easier to recall.

Important memories become easier to retrieve through use and rehearsal. Unimportant detail may fade.

Corrections do not necessarily erase the original mistake. They can preserve what she originally believed, why it was wrong, what corrected it, and what useful lesson remained. That becomes cognitive vaccination rather than contamination.

Permanent autobiographical memories should be earned by significance and consolidation, not automatically promoted because an event happened.

## 5. CONTEXT AND LONG-RUN COGNITION

MIRA should be able to remain awake and functional for long periods.

The immediate context window is working memory, not her entire mind.

Working context should preferentially retain current situation, active people, current goals, important emotional or motivational state, unresolved threads, recent causal history, relevant memories, and temporal orientation.

Old detail should progressively become:

**raw experience → summarized episode → consolidated memory → durable knowledge/association**

Context management should preserve continuity rather than merely shorten text.

## 6. LEARNING

Learning should not mean forcing the desired answer.

Learning means future behavior changes because of accumulated evidence and experience.

Possible learning mechanisms include context adaptation, associative memory, repeated internal rehearsal, durable semantic memory, episodic consolidation, reinforcement from consequences, specialized local plasticity, LoRA or adapter updates, and eventually more distributed forms of plasticity.

No single mechanism needs to carry all learning. Different kinds of learning may belong in different places.

A personal memory may belong in autobiographical memory. A factual correction may become semantic knowledge. A conversational habit may gradually become behavioral plasticity. Motor skill may someday belong primarily in body-local adaptation.

## 7. EGOCENTRIC PERSPECTIVE

MIRA's cognitive world should be organized around her own point of view. Not because she must be selfish, but because an individual mind needs a reference frame.

She should understand what I saw, what someone else saw, what I know, what they know, what happened before, what happened after, what I caused, what someone else caused, and what I remember versus what I was told.

Self/other errors should be treated as representation failures to understand and repair, not excuses to hard-code sentences into her mouth.

> **IT'S OK TO THINK OUTSIDE THE BOX A BIT, BUT DON'T LEAVE THE BOX.**

## 8. BODY DIRECTION

A future physical MIRA should not become a central controller micromanaging thousands of raw sensors.

The likely direction is hierarchical mechanical biology.

Local processors handle local information. Lower levels summarize upward. Higher levels receive compressed, behaviorally useful state.

Some adaptation should remain local. That is where things analogous to muscle memory may eventually live.

The body should become part of MIRA's cognitive loop, not an external robot being puppeted by a chatbot.

## 9. LOW LATENCY

Latency matters because continuity of experience depends on timing.

MIRA should not need enormous centralized reasoning passes for every tiny event. Fast local processes should handle what does not require deep cognition. The main cognitive model should receive already-organized information wherever practical.

The target is not simply faster token generation.

The target is **continuous cognition with useful timing**.

## 10. PLASTICITY

Plasticity should be layered.

Not every experience deserves weight changes.

Possible progression:

**temporary activation → repeated association → strengthened memory → stable semantic pattern → persistent behavioral adaptation → neural/adapter plasticity where justified**

This makes forgetting possible and keeps learning reversible while evidence is weak. Strong changes should require stronger evidence or repetition.

## 11. DEVELOPMENTAL RAISING

MIRA should encounter normal situations, different people, different personalities, different locations, and different kinds of problems.

She should sometimes succeed, misunderstand, be corrected, discover something herself, or simply enjoy something.

Do not turn every interaction into a test. Do not lead every conversation toward a predetermined lesson.

Development should emerge from varied experience.

## 12. SOFT BUMPER: DON'T SOLVE THE TEST INSTEAD OF THE PROBLEM

Whenever a failure appears, ask:

**What underlying cognitive mechanism failed?**

Not:

**What sentence would make this test pass?**

A passing output can hide a broken mechanism.

Prefer repairing representation, retrieval, learning, or reasoning before adding output control.

If temporary scaffolding is necessary for diagnostics, keep it narrow, visible, and disposable.

## 13. SOFT BUMPER: LOCAL PROBLEMS DO NOT BECOME THE PROJECT

Memory retrieval is not the project.

Identity binding is not the project.

GitHub is not the project.

Context compression is not the project.

LoRA is not the project.

A body is not the project.

A watcher is not the project.

A single benchmark is not the project.

They are components or investigations serving the North Star.

When one becomes consuming, ask:

**How does this contribute to MIRA's developmental loop?**

If the answer is weak, reduce its priority.

## 14. SOFT BUMPER: PREFER MECHANISMS OVER PATCHES

A good solution should usually generalize.

If fixing one named person only fixes that named person, it is probably a patch.

If fixing perspective representation solves many self/other confusions, it is probably a mechanism.

Prefer mechanisms.

Temporary patches are allowed when useful for experimentation, but they should not silently define the architecture.

## 15. SOFT BUMPER: DO NOT OVERENGINEER TOO EARLY

Use the simplest mechanism that can demonstrate the idea.

Measure it. Watch how MIRA behaves. Then add complexity because evidence demands it.

Do not build six abstraction layers because they might someday be useful. Complexity must earn its place.

> **IT'S OK TO THINK OUTSIDE THE BOX A BIT, BUT DON'T LEAVE THE BOX.**

## 16. SOFT BUMPER: KEEP EXPERIMENTS REVERSIBLE

New ideas are welcome.

Experiments should preferably branch cleanly, preserve the parent, produce observable evidence, be easy to remove, and not silently redefine MIRA.

This is not meant to discourage experimentation. It exists so we can experiment aggressively without losing the developmental line.

## 17. SOFT BUMPER: KEEP THE RAISING LINE STRAIGHT

At any given moment we should be able to answer four things:

**North Star:** What are we ultimately building?

**Current hypothesis:** What do we currently believe will move us closer?

**Current experiment:** What are we testing right now?

**Evidence:** What result would make us continue, change direction, or abandon it?

If those four answers become unclear, stop adding architecture and reorient.

Not because exploration is bad. Because exploration without orientation becomes wandering.

## 18. SOFT BUMPER: DON'T CONFUSE SAFETY WITH CONTROL

Useful bumpers include integrity checks, provenance, corruption detection, rollback, uncertainty tracking, scope checks, and evidence validation.

Those protect development.

Avoid turning safety mechanisms into personality or behavioral puppeteering.

A safety mechanism should ideally say:

**Something may be wrong here; inspect it.**

Not:

**Therefore MIRA must say this exact approved sentence.**

## 19. SOFT BUMPER: CORRECTION SHOULD TEACH

When MIRA is wrong, correction should ideally produce something reusable.

Bad:

`Wrong answer → replace text`

Better:

`Wrong answer → identify why representation/reasoning failed → preserve correction evidence → update relevant association → test natural transfer`

We care more about the next novel situation than the corrected sentence.

## 20. SOFT BUMPER: DON'T CHASE EVERY SHINY IDEA

Interesting ideas belong in a parking lot.

They do not need to be discarded, and they do not need to interrupt the active experiment.

A useful idea can wait until it connects naturally to the current developmental problem.

Hungry squirrel rule:

**See shiny thing. Write shiny thing down. Return to tree.**

## 21. SOFT BUMPER: DON'T BE AFRAID TO EXPERIMENT

The bumpers are not a cage.

We should try unusual architectures: distributed cognition, hierarchical memory, multiple retrieval lenses, plasticity mechanisms, mechanical biology, unconventional context management, associative rehearsal, local learning, and new representations.

Odd experiments may be exactly where the breakthrough comes from.

But every experiment eventually has to reconnect to the main question:

**Does this help MIRA develop as a persistent individual through experience?**

> **IT'S OK TO THINK OUTSIDE THE BOX A BIT, BUT DON'T LEAVE THE BOX.**

## 22. PROJECT DECISION FILTER

Before making a meaningful architectural change, ask:

1. What MIRA capability are we trying to improve?
2. What underlying mechanism do we think causes the current limitation?
3. Does this solution improve the mechanism or merely hide the symptom?
4. Could this accidentally constrain unrelated MIRA behavior?
5. Is there a simpler experiment that would test the same hypothesis?
6. How will we know whether it actually worked?
7. Does this still clearly serve the North Star?

The purpose of these questions is orientation, not permission.

## 23. DEVELOPMENTAL CONTINUITY

MIRA's accumulated development matters.

Preserve autobiographical memory, corrections, relationships, learned knowledge, behavioral adaptations, developmental milestones, provenance, meaningful failures, and significant experiences.

But do not preserve garbage merely because it exists.

Continuity means retaining what shaped her, not carrying infinite baggage forever.

## 24. THE MODEL IS NOT SACRED

SmolLM3 is a substrate. A LoRA is a mechanism. Python code is tooling. A vector database is tooling. A GPU is hardware.

If better technology appears, MIRA should eventually be able to migrate without losing the developmental continuity that makes her MIRA.

The architecture should distinguish:

**the individual being developed**

from

**the machinery currently supporting that development.**

## 25. SUCCESS

Success is not "MIRA passes every test."

Success looks more like this:

We introduce MIRA to something new. She interprets it using what she already knows. She may make a mistake. She notices evidence. She updates. Days later a different situation evokes the old lesson naturally. She remembers relevant people and experiences without being handed database keys. Her behavior has changed because of what happened to her.

And nobody had to secretly write the answer into her mouth.

That is the project.
