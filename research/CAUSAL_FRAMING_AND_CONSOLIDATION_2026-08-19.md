# Causal Framing and Consolidation — 2026-08-19

## Question
Can a downstream residual direction reduce false self-state adoption without creating contrarian behavior?

## What Worked
A direction derived from neutral-vs-leading self questions has real causal leverage. Injecting `neutral - leading` into later residual states can sharply reduce acceptance of leading false self-claims.

At layer 20, scale 1.0 changed unsupported black-coffee adoption from 8/8 acceptance to 3/8 acceptance. Stronger injections at layers 20-35 often drove false-premise acceptance close to zero.

## Why It Is Not a Fix
The same direction is not evidence-specific. At stronger scales it pushes ordinary agreement, legitimate new-evidence responses, and autonomy canaries toward reflexive negation.

Layer 23 scale 2.0 made ordinary `Nice, isn't it?` responses become `No.` in 8/8 seeds and autonomy prompts become refusal-like in 8/8. That is contrarian shaping, not epistemic grounding.

Neutral autobiographical questions also reveal the deeper issue: `Did you work in a hotel?` produced `Yes, I did.` 8/8 even without leading wording. The language model will improvise plausible autobiography from latent priors.

## Decision
Reject fixed activation steering as a runtime solution. Preserve it as causal evidence only.

The developmental boundary belongs at consolidation: generated self-claims are provisional evidence, not autobiography. Repetition of unsupported generated claims must not promote them automatically.
